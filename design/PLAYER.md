# THE OTHER SIDE — PLAYER DESIGN

## 1. Role

The player controls Shamshir.

Shamshir is a professional systems/security auditor.

He is not a soldier.

## 2. Skills

The game rewards:
- observation;
- memory;
- spatial awareness;
- deduction;
- experimentation;
- patience;
- deliberate decision-making.

## 3. Movement

Movement supports:
- exploration;
- route experimentation;
- observation;
- stealth;
- physical interaction.

It should not become action-combat movement.

## 4. Interaction

Ordinary objects are quick to inspect.

Professionally relevant systems support deeper inspection.

Examples:
- electrical panels;
- terminals;
- cameras;
- relays;
- mechanical systems;
- access systems;
- architectural infrastructure.

## 5. Tools

Tools must be grounded in what an auditor could reasonably carry or use.

Exact implementation inventory remains separate from the world-design lock.

## 6. Notebook

The notebook is a diegetic evidence archive.

It stores:
- observations;
- system notes;
- route annotations;
- evidence;
- technical findings.

It does not automatically solve puzzles.

## 7. Map

Shamshir has architectural/audit plans.

Plans represent intended structure and may be incomplete or outdated.

Physical reality takes precedence.

The player may annotate findings.

## 8. Stealth

Stealth is information management.

The player can:
- reduce exposure;
- vary routes;
- exploit blind spots;
- alter behavior;
- manipulate systems.

## 9. Hiding

Physical concealment avoids observation.

Behavioral concealment breaks established patterns.

Both matter.

## 10. Combat

No conventional combat.

Infrastructure is the primary defensive tool.

## 11. Failure

Failure is systemic containment/interception.

Presentation remains non-graphic.

Reload restores the saved state.

## 12. Progression

Shamshir does not become superhuman.

His progression is intellectual:

**Facility → Mirror → Prediction → Deception → Counter-prediction → Vulnerability**

## 13. Agency

The player must be allowed to:
- test theories;
- make mistakes;
- choose routes;
- interpret evidence;
- discover patterns;
- deceive the Mirror.

The game must not automatically decide what the player should conclude.

The final escape requires the player to connect technician evidence, Mirror behavior and information asymmetry rather than receive an automatic solution.
