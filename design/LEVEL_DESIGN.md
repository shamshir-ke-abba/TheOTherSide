# THE OTHER SIDE — LEVEL DESIGN

## 1. Topology

Rosslyn uses:
- a central circulation structure;
- layered loops;
- controlled progression gates;
- vertical cores;
- service routes;
- genuine blind spots.

## 2. Canonical Graph

### Ground

Exterior → R-101 → R-102 → HUB-00 → R-103

### B-1 West

HUB-B1 → C-202 → R-201 → C-203 → VC-03

### B-1 East

HUB-B1 → R-202 / R-301 → R-302

R-201 and R-202 are sealed from each other.

### B-2

VC-01 → R-401 → C-403 → R-402 → R-403 → C-499 → Exterior

R-403 also connects to VC-03.

### +1

R-501 ↔ R-502

## 3. Verticality

VC-01 is the primary stair.

VC-02 is the service elevator and terminates at B-1.

VC-03 is the air-gapped maintenance shaft.

Vertical routes must remain physically legible.

## 4. Circulation

Major routes use approximately 2.4 m width.

Secondary research/office routes target 1.8 m.

Maintenance routes target 1.2 m.

## 5. Sightlines

Sightlines should be intentionally designed around:
- observation;
- occlusion;
- thresholds;
- corners;
- controlled openings;
- vertical relationships.

Do not create arbitrary sightline blockers.

## 6. Acoustic Relationships

Acoustic exposure must follow architecture and infrastructure.

Mechanical noise can mask or reveal activity.

Acoustic isolation can create genuine information boundaries.

## 7. Progression

Story controls access to new sectors.

The player controls behavior inside accessible sectors.

## 8. Revisit Design

Revisits should alter:
- surveillance;
- lighting;
- access;
- system operation;
- sound;
- Mirror expectations.

The underlying architecture remains recognizable.

## 9. Final Route

The final escape uses the B-2 legacy route and exits through C-499.

It must be possible because of established spatial and system logic, not because a new hallway appears.
