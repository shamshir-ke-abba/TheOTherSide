# THE OTHER SIDE — ART DIRECTION

## 1. Visual Goal

Rosslyn should look like a believable research facility first and a horror location second.

The horror comes from context, system behavior and changing conditions.

## 2. Architecture

Use grounded mid-century-to-late-industrial institutional architecture.

Avoid:
- glossy futuristic laboratories;
- excessive holograms;
- impossible structures;
- decorative sci-fi geometry.

## 3. Materials

Primary material families:
- painted concrete;
- institutional plaster;
- metal;
- glass;
- industrial flooring;
- service conduits;
- aging mechanical hardware;
- paper and analogue documentation.

## 4. Digital / Analog Contrast

Modern digital systems coexist with older infrastructure.

Digital equipment should feel maintained and functional.

Analog systems should feel old but physically understandable.

## 5. Lighting

Lighting is practical and architectural.

Sources include:
- institutional fixtures;
- emergency lighting;
- equipment indicators;
- service lighting;
- handheld inspection light.

## 6. Props

Props should communicate function.

Do not fill rooms with arbitrary clutter.

Every significant prop should either:
- establish occupation;
- explain a system;
- support investigation;
- reinforce history;
- communicate a state change.

## 7. Mirror Visual Language

Early:
- reflection;
- indirect artifacts;
- unexplained environmental response.

Middle:
- ambiguous silhouettes/presence;
- altered relationships between objects and observation.

Late:
- physical obstruction/presence;
- still not a conventional monster.

## 8. Environmental Decay

Decay should be systemic and restrained.

Use:
- abandoned work;
- interrupted procedures;
- emergency signage;
- displaced equipment;
- maintenance traces.

Avoid gore as a primary storytelling method.

## 9. Exterior

Pacific Northwest coastal forest.

Overcast weather transitions toward rain and coastal fog.

The perimeter is functional and security-focused.
